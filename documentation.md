# Island Economy — Complete 12-Round Edition
## Full Story-to-Term Reference & Government Scenarios

---

## (15 Economic Terms)

| Stage | Event in the game | Economic term(s) | One-line definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2–3 people on the island, each producing everything alone | **Autarky / self-sufficiency** | Producing all you consume yourself, with zero trade. |
| 2 | A basic rule about who owns what gets established | **Property rights** | Recognized claims over resources/output — the precondition that makes voluntary exchange possible. |
| 3 | They start exchanging goods/services with each other | **Division of labor**; **Gains from trade**; **Comparative advantage** | Specializing in what you're relatively best at, then trading, makes everyone better off. |
| 4 | A 4th and 5th person arrive, bringing new labor/skills | **Labor force growth** | More workers = more potential output and more possible trades. |
| 5 | Their arrival enables narrower, more efficient specialization | **Extent of the market** (Adam Smith); **Productivity gains from specialization** | "Division of labor is limited by the extent of the market" — more trading partners allow narrower specialization. |
| 6 | A 6th person introduces gold to replace direct barter | **Medium of exchange**; **Commodity money**; **Double coincidence of wants** | Gold adopted as universally accepted good-for-trading; removes need to find someone wanting exactly what you have. |
| 7 | That person holds gold and issues claims against it | **Financial intermediary**; **Warehouse banking / gold depository** | Someone stores commodity money and issues paper claims on it. |
| 8 | The banker introduces paper notes redeemable in gold | **Representative money**; **Gold standard** | Paper has no intrinsic value — valuable only as a redeemable claim on gold. |
| 9 | Someone borrows gold/notes to build a tool instead of consuming today | **Capital investment / capital formation**; **Interest**; **Time preference** | Giving up present consumption to build a tool that raises future output; interest prices the time value of money. |
| 10 | Entrepreneurs risk capital on new tools/methods that cut production costs | **Productivity gain / technological progress**; **Entrepreneurship** (Schumpeter) | Same labor now produces more output — shifting the supply curve right and lowering market prices. |
| 11 | People consume more of that cheaper item | **Law of demand**; **Price elasticity of demand**; **Engel's Law** | Lower price → higher quantity demanded; as income rises, budget share on food falls. |
| 12 | The banker issues more notes than gold actually held | **Fractional-reserve banking**; **Credit expansion** | Issuing more claims than reserves back; funds booms before trust collapses. |
| 13 | Prices climb as money supply outpaces goods | **Inflation**; **Quantity theory of money** (MV = PQ) | Too much paper chases the same goods; purchasing power of money falls. |
| 14 | A second island or trading ship appears | **External trade**; **Comparative advantage at scale**; **Exchange rates** | Specialization logic reapplied between two whole communities; different money needs exchange rates. |
| 15 | A shared resource (e.g. fishing ground) comes under strain from overuse | **Tragedy of the commons** | A resource nobody owns individually gets over-exploited because no one bears the full cost of their own use. |

## Part 2 — Government Point-of-View: Eighteen Outcome Scenarios

# Island Economy — 18 Endings with Country Examples

---

## **E1 — The Prosperous Republic** ⭐

All institutions working perfectly. Exponential growth, low inequality, high stability.

**Country example: Singapore**
- GDP/capita: $72,000
- Growth: ~7% annually
- Inequality (Gini): 0.42 (low)
- Stability: 58 years without major crisis
- All economic terms functioning at full capacity

---

## **E2 — Prosperous but Insular**

Internal economy perfect. No external trade. Growth rapid but capped below E1.

**Country example: Japan (1980s–1990s)**
- GDP/capita: $39,000
- Growth: 4–5% annually (high, but capped)
- Inequality (Gini): 0.30 (very low)
- Stability: High
- Strong manufacturing, capital formation, banking—but protectionist trade policy limits growth ceiling

---

## **E3 — Commons Crisis**

Perfect institutions until shared resources collapse. Growth reverses catastrophically.

**Country example: Easter Island (historical)**
- Population: Collapsed from ~15,000 to <2,000
- Growth: Exponential until resource collapse
- Commons: Deforestation destroyed fishing grounds and food supply
- Result: Societal collapse, warfare, starvation

---

## **E4 — Inflation Crisis → Reset to Barter**

Over-issue money. Trust shatters. Currency destroyed. Hyperinflation. Revert to barter.

**Country example: Venezuela (2012–present)**
- GDP/capita: $3,000 (down 75% from peak)
- Hyperinflation: 2,600% annually (2020)
- Currency collapses: 3 resets in 10 years
- Dollarization: Population switched to USD for transactions
- Result: Partial return to barter; capital formation stopped

---

## **E5 — Stagnation: No Property Rights**

No property rights established. Nobody specializes. Permanent autarky.

**Country example: North Korea**
- GDP/capita: ~$1,300
- Growth: Flat (no real growth since 1990s)
- Specialization: Minimal; households produce everything
- Trade: Virtually none (isolated)
- Property rights: Only state owns everything; citizens have no claims

---

## **E6 — Extractive Elite State**

Property rights only for ruling clique. Elite thrive, most at subsistence.

**Country example: Saudi Arabia (1970s–1990s)**
- Oligarchic control: Al Saud family controls oil wealth
- Growth: Moderate (elite only benefit)
- Inequality (Gini): 0.45 (high)
- Specialization: Only for connected merchants and firms
- Most population: Subsistence wages despite oil wealth

---

## **E7 — Closed Barter Economy**

No money adopted. Specialization happens but throttled. Real but slow growth.

**Country example: Medieval Europe (pre-1200)**
- Specialization: Craftspeople, knights, monks—but limited by double coincidence of wants
- Trade volume: Highly restricted (barter required)
- Growth: Slow, steady
- Money (rare coins): Existed but underutilized
- Result: Stable but permanently low income ceiling

---

## **E8 — Fiat Overreach Crisis → Loop to Money Choice**

Decree unbacked paper money. Trust collapses on first shock. Must re-choose money system.

**Country example: Argentina (2001–2002)**
- Peso pegged to dollar (hard money), then abandoned
- Government decreed 1 peso = 1 dollar (unsustainable without backing)
- Bank runs (2001): ATMs ran dry, peso devalued 75% overnight
- Reversion: Returned to peso under controlled conditions, then stabilized
- Result: Crisis forced monetary policy rethink

---

## **E9 — Crony Trade (Divided Island)**

Trade open only to favored subgroup. Growth for few, stagnation for most.

**Country example: Russia (1990s–2020s)**
- Oligarchy: Berezovsky, Khodorkovsky, Abramovich control major exports
- Comparative advantage: Exploited by connected merchants only
- Ordinary Russians: Exclude from capital access, licenses, major trade
- Growth: Aggregate GDP looks moderate; distribution is terrible
- Gini: 0.41 (high); top 1% holds 35% of wealth

---

## **E10 — Capital Starvation**

Ban banking outright. Nobody can borrow. Productivity stuck.

**Country example: Papua New Guinea (modern)**
- Banking access: <10% of population
- Credit markets: Virtually nonexistent
- Productivity gains: Blocked (no tools can be financed)
- Growth: Slow and limited to subsistence
- Capital investment: Nearly impossible

---

## **E11 — Failed Entrepreneurship**

Suppress entrepreneurship with taxes/licensing. Productivity flat.

**Country example: Soviet Union (1960s–1980s)**
- Innovation: Suppressed by state monopoly on production
- Entrepreneurship: Illegal; entrepreneurs imprisoned or killed
- Productivity: Flat after 1970s (no new tools/methods adopted)
- Growth: Stagnated while West accelerated
- Result: Economic collapse, unable to compete technologically

---

## **E12 — Demand Collapse**

Productivity surges but consumption suppressed. Goods pile up unsold. Agricultural collapse.

**Country example: Soviet Union (1990–1991)**
- Perestroika: Loosened prices; production surged temporarily
- Central planning collapsed: Supply existed but demand destruction (collapse of command system)
- Food crisis: Farmers lost incentive to produce; rationing began
- Growth reversed: Real output fell 14% in 1991–1992
- Result: Shortage of goods despite prior abundance

---

## **E13 — Isolationist Relapse → Loop Back to Autarky**

Mandate self-sufficiency quotas. Dismantle specialization. Revert to autarky.

**Country example: China (Cultural Revolution, 1966–1976)**
- Mao's communes: Ordered self-sufficiency by village
- Specialization destroyed: Factories, merchants, scholars sent to farms to grow food
- Trade stopped: Each commune self-contained (attempted)
- Result: Great Famine (1959–1962); tens of millions died
- Reverted to subsistence production; economy near collapse

---

## **E14 — Fragmented Currencies**

Two economies, incompatible currencies, no exchange rate mechanism. Trade fails.

**Country example: Pre-EU Europe (1970s–1980s)**
- Multiple currencies: Deutsche Mark, Franc, Lira, Peseta, etc. with volatile exchange rates
- Trade inefficiency: Price signals didn't translate; traders faced massive exchange risk
- Comparative advantage unrealized: Could theoretically trade but didn't due to currency incompatibility
- Result: Trade stayed below potential; created impetus for European monetary union (1999 euro launch)

---

## **E15 — Overexploited Commons → Partial Recovery**

Regulate commons too late. Partial collapse, then slow recovery.

**Country example: Iceland (North Atlantic Cod Fishery, 1970s–2000s)**
- Overfishing (1960s–1970s): Cod stocks crashed despite warning signs
- Late regulation (1980s+): Quotas imposed after stocks near-collapse
- Partial recovery: Stocks recovered 50% by 2000, but took 30 years
- Lost decade: 1980s saw depression, high unemployment, emigration
- Result: Now stable but growth never recovered peak 1970s levels

---

## **E16 — Regulatory Capture (Hidden Extraction)**

Rules exist on paper but officials ignore them for insiders. Growth looks healthy, stability fragile.

**Country example: China (2000s–present)**
- SOE corruption: State-owned enterprises exempt from regulations applied to private firms
- Hidden extraction: Officials redirect resources to connected firms
- Aggregate growth: Looks like E1 (~9% GDP growth)
- Fragility: Trust erodes silently; whistle-blowers punished
- Result: System looks sound externally; internal inequality and captured regulation building resentment

---

## **E17 — Hyper-Deflation / Money Trap**

Shrink money supply. Prices fall continuously. People hoard money instead of spending/investing.

**Country example: Japan (Lost Decade, 1990s–2000s)**
- Deflation: Prices fell 1–2% annually for 15+ years
- Hoarding: Consumers delayed purchases, expecting lower prices next year
- Investment collapse: Firms couldn't borrow profitably; interest rates near zero
- Growth: Stagnation (~0.5% annually)
- Result: Economy trapped in low-output equilibrium despite sound institutions

---

## **E18 — Subsistence Equilibrium with Limited Trade**

All institutions built, modest trade, commons protected. Steady, realistic growth.

**Country example: South Korea (1960s–1980s)**
- Institutions: Property rights, banking, capital formation all working
- Modest trade: Limited external trade initially; gradual opening
- Commons management: Regulated early
- Growth: Solid ~6% annually
- Result: Stable, mixed-outcome economy; neither perfect E1 nor troubled

# Island Economy — The Game
## A Playable Story in 15 Rounds

---

## ROUND 1: THE ISLAND
**Year 1. Population: 3 people. Condition: Everyone produces everything alone.**

You are the government. Three islanders—Alex, Blake, and Casey—each hunt, fish, build shelter, and gather alone. They produce enough to survive, but barely. Life is subsistence.

A storm destroys Casey's shelter. Alex and Blake could help, but there's no rule about who owns what. Blake asks: should we help our neighbor? Should we establish *rules about ownership*?

**CHOICE:**
- **A) Establish property rights — everyone owns what they produce.** → ROUND 2
- **B) Leave everything open-access — no ownership rules.** → E5 (STOP)
- **C) Grant all property to Alex and Blake only; Casey owns nothing.** → E6 (STOP)

---

## ROUND 2: THE TRADE
**Year 2. Population: 3. Condition: Property rights established.**

Blake realizes: "I'm good at fishing. I hate hunting. Alex hates fishing but loves hunting."

Blake proposes: "I fish. You hunt. We trade." 

But the government has a choice: allow this, or require everyone to produce everything themselves?

**CHOICE:**
- **A) Allow free trade and specialization.** → ROUND 3
- **B) Require self-sufficiency quotas; forbid specialization.** → E13 (STOP)
- **C) Only allow Alex and Blake to trade; Casey must stay alone.** → E9 (STOP)

---

## ROUND 3: GROWTH
**Year 3. Population: 5 (two new arrivals). Condition: Free trade established.**

Two new people—Dana and Elliot—arrive. With 5 people, specialization deepens. Output per person rises 30%.

But barter is messy: "I have 20 fish. I want tools. But the tool-maker wants meat, not fish."

The government must decide: adopt a *medium of exchange* to replace barter?

**CHOICE:**
- **A) Adopt gold as money.** → ROUND 4
- **B) Keep barter system.** → E7 (STOP)
- **C) Decree paper money (no gold backing).** → E8 (STOP)

---

## ROUND 4: BANKING
**Year 5. Population: 5. Condition: Gold money adopted.**

Gold works. Trade volume triples.

Elliot (the trader) says: "People are burying gold at home. I'll hold it and give them receipts."

Elliot opens a bank. The government must decide: regulate banking, or let it run free?

**CHOICE:**
- **A) License the bank and require reserves (50% gold backing).** → ROUND 5
- **B) License the bank but never enforce reserves.** → E4 (STOP)
- **C) Ban banking outright.** → E10 (STOP)

---

## ROUND 5: CAPITAL
**Year 7. Population: 5. Condition: Regulated banking.**

Casey says: "I want to build a fishing net. It costs 100 gold. I have 50. Can I borrow?"

Elliot agrees. Casey builds it. Fishing output explodes 5×.

The government must decide: support entrepreneurs, or suppress them?

**CHOICE:**
- **A) Support entrepreneurs; allow low-tax borrowing.** → ROUND 6
- **B) Suppress entrepreneurs with high taxes.** → E11 (STOP)
- **C) Only allow elite to borrow.** → E6 (STOP)

---

## ROUND 6: PRODUCTIVITY SURGE
**Year 10. Population: 5. Condition: Entrepreneurship supported.**

Casey's net works. Blake builds better hooks. Dana makes better ropes. Elliot invents a fish smoker.

Tools improve. Costs fall 40%. At lower prices, people buy more. Consumption rises.

The government must control *inflation*: keep money supply aligned with real output.

**CHOICE:**
- **A) Enforce strict reserve requirements.** → ROUND 7
- **B) Over-tighten reserves; shrink money supply.** → E17 (STOP)
- **C) Ignore reserves; let banks issue unlimited money.** → E4 (STOP)

---

## ROUND 7: STABILITY
**Year 12. Population: 5. Condition: Reserves enforced; money stable.**

Five years of 7% growth. Real wages up 40%. Island prospers.

Pressure appears: the *fishing ground* is getting crowded. Fish stocks falling. Soon it will collapse.

The government must decide: regulate the fishing ground before collapse, or leave it open?

**CHOICE:**
- **A) Regulate now; set fishing quotas.** → ROUND 8A
- **B) Leave it open; let people fish freely.** → E3 (STOP)
- **C) Regulate late (after partial collapse).** → E15 (STOP)

---

## ROUND 8A: PROTECTED COMMONS
**Year 13. Population: 5. Condition: Commons regulated.**

Government sets quotas. Each person max 200 fish/year. Heavy fines for violations.

Fishery stays healthy. Five years later, fish populations stable.

A merchant ship appears. They're from another island with tropical fruits, spices, copper. They want to trade.

The government must decide: open external trade, or stay closed?

**CHOICE:**
- **A) Open trade freely.** → ROUND 9A
- **B) Close borders; protect local producers.** → E2 (STOP)
- **C) Open trade, but currencies incompatible (no exchange rates).** → E14 (STOP)

---

## ROUND 9A: EXTERNAL TRADE
**Year 14. Population: 5. Condition: Open trade with other island.**

Ships arrive regularly. Island specializes in processed fish. Other island specializes in fruit. Both prosper.

Comparative advantage applies at the *island level*. Incomes rise 30%.

Elliot handles currency exchange. Your gold trades for the other island's silver.

The government faces final choice: maintain all regulations, or relax them for insiders?

**CHOICE:**
- **A) Maintain all regulations; stay disciplined.** → **E1 ⭐ (GAME OVER: The Prosperous Republic)**
- **B) Relax regulations; let officials exempt insiders.** → **E16 (GAME OVER: Regulatory Capture)**

## HOW TO PLAY

1. **Start at ROUND 1**
2. **Read the scenario**
3. **Choose A, B, or C**
4. **Follow the path**
5. **When you reach an ending, the game is over**

**Most players take 8–9 decisions before reaching an ending.**

**Only path A all the way (9 decisions) → E1 (The Prosperous Republic) ⭐**

**Try again and pick different choices to reach other endings!**
