# Island Economy — Complete 12-Round Edition
## Full Story-to-Term Reference & Government Scenarios

---

## Part 1 — Story-to-Term Reference Table (15 Economic Terms)

| Stage | Event in the game | Economic term(s) | One-line definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2–3 people on the island, each producing everything alone | **Autarky / self-sufficiency** | Producing all you consume yourself, with zero trade. |
| 2 | A basic rule about who owns what gets established | **Property rights** | Recognized claims over resources/output — the precondition that makes voluntary exchange possible. |
| 3 | They start exchanging goods/services with each other | **Division of labor**; **Gains from trade**; **Comparative advantage** | Specializing in what you're relatively best at, then trading, makes everyone better off. |
| 4 | A 4th and 5th person arrive, bringing new labor/skills | **Labor force growth** | More workers = more potential output and more possible trades. |
| 5 | Their arrival enables narrower, more efficient specialization | **Extent of the market** (Adam Smith); **Productivity gains from specialization** | "Division of labor is limited by the extent of the market" — more trading partners allow narrower specialization. |
| 6 | A 6th person introduces gold to replace direct barter | **Medium of exchange**; **Commodity money**; **Double coincidence of wants** | Gold adopted as universally accepted good-for-trading; removes need to find someone wanting exactly what you have. |
| 7 | That person holds gold and issues claims against it | **Financial intermediary**; **Warehouse banking / gold depository** | Someone stores commodity money and issues paper claims on it. |
| 8 | The banker introduces paper notes redeemable in gold | **Representative money**; **Gold standard** | Paper has no intrinsic value — valuable only as a redeemable claim on gold. |
| 9 | Someone borrows gold/notes to build a tool instead of consuming today | **Capital investment / capital formation**; **Interest**; **Time preference** | Giving up present consumption to build a tool that raises future output; interest prices the time value of money. |
| 10 | Entrepreneurs risk capital on new tools/methods that cut production costs | **Productivity gain / technological progress**; **Entrepreneurship** (Schumpeter) | Same labor now produces more output — shifting the supply curve right and lowering market prices. |
| 11 | People consume more of that cheaper item | **Law of demand**; **Price elasticity of demand**; **Engel's Law** | Lower price → higher quantity demanded; as income rises, budget share on food falls. |
| 12 | The banker issues more notes than gold actually held | **Fractional-reserve banking**; **Credit expansion** | Issuing more claims than reserves back; funds booms before trust collapses. |
| 13 | Prices climb as money supply outpaces goods | **Inflation**; **Quantity theory of money** (MV = PQ) | Too much paper chases the same goods; purchasing power of money falls. |
| 14 | A second island or trading ship appears | **External trade**; **Comparative advantage at scale**; **Exchange rates** | Specialization logic reapplied between two whole communities; different money needs exchange rates. |
| 15 | A shared resource (e.g. fishing ground) comes under strain from overuse | **Tragedy of the commons** | A resource nobody owns individually gets over-exploited because no one bears the full cost of their own use. |

## Part 2 — Government Point-of-View: Eighteen Outcome Scenarios

### **E1 — The Prosperous Republic** ⭐ (BEST ENDING)
**Perfect path through all 12 rounds.** Protect property rights → Free specialization → Full market extent → Gold money → Regulated banking → Sound representative money → Entrepreneurship & capital → Technological progress → Free consumer markets → Enforce reserves & control inflation → Open external trade → Regulate commons

Every institution built in the right order, none abused. Growth compounds exponentially at every stage. All 15 economic terms functioning at full capacity. Low inequality, high stability, maximum wealth creation. This is the textbook economy.

**Growth trajectory:** Exponential | **Inequality:** Low | **Stability:** Very high | **Key strength:** Institutional complementarity

---

### **E2 — Prosperous but Insular**
**Path:** Perfect through R11, but closes doors to external trade (R11 decision)

Complete sound internal economy — money, banking, capital, all working flawlessly. All terms functioning internally. But island never captures gains from trading with a second community. Growth is rapid but capped at roughly 40% below E1's trajectory. Low inequality, high stability, but lower absolute wealth and no exchange-rate complexity.

**Growth trajectory:** High (capped) | **Inequality:** Low | **Stability:** High | **Key weakness:** Forgone external comparative advantage

---

### **E3 — Commons Crisis**
**Path:** Perfect through R11 (external trade opens), but commons never regulated (R12 decision)

Every institution perfect until the final moment. Fishing ground gets fished out catastrophically. Four rounds of exponential growth erode from underneath as the resource collapses. Massive productivity drop as primary food source vanishes. Growth reverses sharply in final years.

**Growth trajectory:** Exponential rise, then collapse | **Inequality:** Rises as crisis hits | **Stability:** Catastrophic failure | **Key lesson:** Market failures require regulation

---

### **E4 — Inflation Crisis → Reset to Barter**
**Path:** Protect rights → Free trade → Gold money → Licensed bank → Entrepreneurship → **Reserves never enforced (R10 decision)**

All systems working until R10. Banker issues far more notes than gold held. Extra "credit" funds a short-lived investment boom and unsustainable consumption. Prices start rising (inflation visible). Trust in paper money shatters. Island reverts to barter overnight. Fractional-reserve banking term appears but catastrophically distorted (⚠). **A single Round 10 choice unwinds 9 rounds of progress.**

**Growth trajectory:** Boom then sudden crash | **Inequality:** Extreme during crisis | **Stability:** Volatile/chaotic | **Key lesson:** Reserve enforcement is load-bearing

---

### **E5 — Stagnation: No Property Rights**
**Path:** Reject property rights (R1 decision) — permanent loop at R1

Without secure claims, nobody specializes or trades — anything beyond immediate consumption gets seized. Permanent autarky even as population grows. Per-capita output flat forever. Only term fully functioning is autarky itself; all others blocked or stunted.

**Growth trajectory:** None | **Inequality:** N/A (all subsistence) | **Stability:** Unstable (revolts, reform attempts) | **Key lesson:** Property rights are foundational

---

### **E6 — Extractive Elite State**
**Path:** Grant property rights only to ruling clique (R1 decision)

Elite get full access to trade, banking, capital, entrepreneurship. Everyone else works land/tools they don't own. Thin layer of specialization, money, and trade exists among the rulers; the rest stay in subsistence autarky. Aggregate numbers look moderate but hide a distribution collapse. Most population see zero gains.

**Growth trajectory:** Moderate (elite only) | **Inequality:** Extreme | **Stability:** Unstable (revolts) | **Key lesson:** Inclusive institutions matter

---

### **E7 — Closed Barter Economy**
**Path:** Protect rights → Free trade → **Reject gold; stay on barter (R4 decision)**

Specialization keeps happening, but every exchange still needs a double coincidence of wants. Trade stays capped well below potential. Real, permanent growth but severely throttled. Never advances to banking or capital formation; can never reach rounds 5+.

**Growth trajectory:** Slow steady (hits ceiling) | **Inequality:** Low | **Stability:** High (but low equilibrium) | **Key lesson:** Medium of exchange is enabling technology

---

### **E8 — Fiat Overreach Crisis → Loop to R4**
**Path:** Protect rights → Free trade → **Decree unbacked paper token before banking trust exists (R4 decision)**

Fiat decree bypasses the gold step. Token accepted only as long as decree's credibility holds. No intrinsic value, no gold backing, no earned trust. First serious shock (bad harvest, external rumor) breaks that trust. Money collapses. Island loops back to R4, forced to choose between gold and barter again from scratch.

**Growth trajectory:** Collapse and retry | **Inequality:** Volatile | **Stability:** Very unstable | **Key lesson:** Trust in currency must be earned

---

### **E9 — Crony Trade (Divided Island)**
**Path:** Protect rights → **License trade only to favored subgroup (R2 decision)**

Comparative advantage gets exploited, but only by license-holders; the rest of island functionally stays autarkic. Growth is real for connected merchants and zero for everyone else. Aggregate numbers can look moderate while most population sees no benefit. Resentment and inequality rise.

**Growth trajectory:** Moderate (top only) | **Inequality:** Extreme | **Stability:** Unstable (resentment) | **Key lesson:** Inclusive markets are more stable

---

### **E10 — Capital Starvation**
**Path:** Rights → Trade → Gold → Banking → **Ban banking outright (R5 decision)**

Wary of banker abuse, government forbids all financial intermediation. Nobody can borrow to build the tool that would raise productivity. Capital investment term is blocked entirely (✗). Growth stalls at comfortable but permanently lower ceiling. Productive potential never unlocked.

**Growth trajectory:** Plateau (no capital) | **Inequality:** Low | **Stability:** High but rigid | **Key lesson:** Capital markets enable growth

---

### **E11 — Failed Entrepreneurship**
**Path:** Rights → Trade → Gold → Banking → Representative money → **Suppress entrepreneurship with heavy taxation/licensing (R8 decision)**

All institutional foundations exist, but government discourages risk-taking with high startup taxes or approval delays. Nobody has incentive to build new tools or methods. Productivity stays flat even though capital exists. Growth stalls at modest level. Entrepreneurship and productivity terms appear but crippled (⚠).

**Growth trajectory:** Slow (no tech) | **Inequality:** Low | **Stability:** High | **Key lesson:** Entrepreneurship requires incentives

---

### **E12 — Demand Collapse**
**Path:** Rights → Trade → Gold → Banking → Capital → Entrepreneurship → **Suppress consumer spending with taxes/rationing (R9 decision)**

Productivity surge happens; goods become abundant and cheap. But government bans or heavily taxes consumption of newly abundant goods. Goods pile up unsold. Farmers lose incentive to plant next season. Agricultural collapse. Manufacturing without demand creates waste. Growth reverses.

**Growth trajectory:** Rise then sudden reverse | **Inequality:** Rising as crisis hits | **Stability:** Economic crisis | **Key lesson:** Demand is as important as supply

---

### **E13 — Isolationist Relapse → Loop to R1**
**Path:** Rights → **Mandate self-sufficiency quotas, dismantling division of labor (R2 decision)**

Government orders households to produce everything themselves. Specialization that had formed gets actively dismantled. Households forced back to autarky. **Loops back to R1/origin.** A single bad policy in Round 2 unwinds the foundation and resets the game entirely.

**Growth trajectory:** Collapse and reset | **Inequality:** Rising as specialization breaks | **Stability:** Chaotic reset | **Key lesson:** Path dependency is real

---

### **E14 — Fragmented Currencies**
**Path:** Rights → Trade → Gold → Banking → Capital → Entrepreneurship → Markets → Inflation controlled → **Second island uses incompatible currency, no exchange rate standard (R11 decision)**

Trade opportunity appears but second island uses completely incompatible currency with no agreed exchange mechanism. Price signals don't translate between islands. Comparative advantage exists theoretically but can't be realized. Trade fails. Weak bilateral barter instead of efficient market. External trade term appears distorted (⚠).

**Growth trajectory:** Moderate plateau | **Inequality:** Low (internally) | **Stability:** Frustrating but stable | **Key lesson:** Exchange rates and currency compatibility matter

---

### **E15 — Overexploited Commons / Partial Recovery**
**Path:** Perfect through R11, but commons regulation comes **too late or too weak (R12 decision)**

Growth proceeds beautifully until the final round. Fish stock crashes after decades of overfishing before regulation kicks in. Government steps in with emergency quotas. Partial recovery possible over years, but years of lost productivity and wealth destruction. Scenario between E1 (full success) and E3 (collapse). Recovers but never fully.

**Growth trajectory:** Rise, crisis, partial recovery | **Inequality:** Moderate (rises during crisis) | **Stability:** Volatile but self-correcting | **Key lesson:** Prevention is cheaper than cure

---

### **E16 — Regulatory Capture (Hidden Extraction)**
**Path:** All institutions built correctly on paper, but captured by insiders (R10 decision: reserve enforcement is gamed)**

Government thinks it enforces reserves, regulates banking, and controls inflation. But captured regulators let favored banks ignore reserve requirements. Private gains, public costs. Looks exactly like E1 from aggregate numbers. Fragile — trust in institutions erodes quietly. First shock reveals the structure was hollow.

**Growth trajectory:** Moderate (hidden costs) | **Inequality:** Extreme (hidden) | **Stability:** Vulnerable to shock | **Key lesson:** Institutional capture is invisible until crisis

---

### **E17 — Hyper-Deflation / Money Trap**
**Path:** Rights → Trade → Gold → Banking → Capital → Entrepreneurship → Markets → **Over-enforce reserves; shrink money supply (R10 decision)**

Government so afraid of inflation it bans fractional reserves entirely and contracts money supply. Prices fall continuously. People hoard money instead of spending or investing. Capital formation collapses. Entrepreneurs can't borrow because money is becoming more valuable every year. Economy stalls in deflationary trap.

**Growth trajectory:** Stagnation | **Inequality:** Moderate (nominal inequality grows) | **Stability:** Deflationary trap | **Key lesson:** Too little money is as harmful as too much

---

### **E18 — Subsistence Equilibrium with Limited Trade**
**Path:** Perfect institutions through R11, engage modest external trade (R11), protect commons (R12)**

Build all 12 rounds successfully but choose cautious middle path on external trade—modest engagement rather than full opening. All institutions functional, commons protected. Mid-tier outcome. Faster growth than E2/E7 but slower than E1. Stable, realistic, mixed-outcomes scenario. Represents a "good but not perfect" economy.

**Growth trajectory:** Solid steady | **Inequality:** Low-moderate | **Stability:** High | **Key lesson:** Middle paths can be stable

## Part 4 — The Twelve-Round Decision Tree

**Round 0 — Origin:** 2–3 islanders, autarky, nothing owned.

### **Round 1 — Property Rights (Foundation)**
**Term: Property rights**

The first and most fundamental choice. Without it, nothing else works.

- **1A:** Protect property rights for all → **Round 2**  
  Secure claims over resources established for every islander.
- **1B:** Leave resources open-access → **E5 Stagnation** (loop: stays at R1)  
  No incentive to specialize; permanent autarky.
- **1C:** Grant rights only to ruling clique → **E6 Extractive Elite** (loop: revolution retries R1)  
  Elite capture; subsistence masses; structural inequality.

---

### **Round 2 — Specialization & Trade (First Gains)**
**Terms: Division of labor, gains from trade, comparative advantage**

(From 1A)

- **2A:** Allow free specialization & trade → **Round 3**  
  Comparative advantage emerges; each person does what they're best at.
- **2B:** Restrict trade to licensed merchants → **E9 Crony Trade**  
  Growth benefits only connected traders; most stay autarkic.
- **2C:** Mandate self-sufficiency quotas → **E13 Isolationist Relapse** (loops back to R1)  
  Dismantle specialization; revert to autarky.

---

### **Round 3 — Market Extent (Scaling)**
**Terms: Extent of the market, productivity gains from specialization**

(From 2A)

- **3A:** Open channels for broader specialization → **Round 4**  
  Island is ready to adopt a medium of exchange; specialization deepens.
- **3B:** Restrict market size / keep specialization limited → stays in **limited growth mode**, eventually leads to **E7 (if continuing)** or transition to R4 with friction.
  Economy develops but never reaches full potential.
- **3C:** Encourage even deeper specialization (subsistence → craft → niche focus) → **Round 4** (with bonus productivity boost)  
  Sets up faster rounds ahead.

---

### **Round 4 — Medium of Exchange (Money Emerges)**
**Terms: Medium of exchange, commodity money, double coincidence of wants**

(From 3A)

- **4A:** Legalize gold as money → **Round 5**  
  Gold becomes universally accepted; double coincidence solved; trade accelerates.
- **4B:** Reject money, stay on barter → **E7 Closed Barter Economy**  
  Specialization keeps happening but trade stays throttled; never advances to banking.
- **4C:** Decree fiat paper token (bypass gold) → **E8 Fiat Overreach** (loops back to R4)  
  No organic trust; first shock shatters confidence; must re-choose between gold and barter.

---

### **Round 5 — Financial Intermediaries (Banking Emerges)**
**Terms: Financial intermediary, warehouse banking, representative money**

(From 4A)

Someone recognizes an opportunity: hold gold for others, issue paper claims. Trust-based banking starts.

- **5A:** License and encourage banking → **Round 6**  
  Financial intermediation begins; deposits and claims on gold enable more flexible credit.
- **5B:** Restrict banking severely / require 100% reserves only → stalls the system; either loops back or leads to **E10 (Capital Starvation)** if capital formation is later blocked.  
  No fractional reserves means no capital formation leverage.
- **5C:** Ban banking outright → **E10 Capital Starvation**  
  No borrowing; no tools; productivity stuck; capital term blocked (✗).

---

### **Round 6 — Representative Money (Trust in Paper)**
**Terms: Representative money, gold standard, trust in currency**

(From 5A)

The banker's paper claims (now called "notes") are accepted as good as gold.

- **6A:** Establish gold-backed paper standard → **Round 7**  
  Notes circulate, redeemable in gold; trust in currency becomes institutional; interest rates emerge.
- **6B:** Over-regulate notes or force deflation (too little paper in circulation) → lowers trade volume; friction grows; eventual stagnation if R7+ choices are poor.
  Money exists but is too scarce; hoarding incentives appear.
- **6C:** Start issuing unbacked paper claims (too many notes vs. gold) → sets up **R10 crisis** later if reserves aren't enforced. Money appears sound now, but cracks form.

---

### **Round 7 — Capital & Borrowing (Time Preference)**
**Terms: Capital investment, interest, time preference**

(From 6A)

With paper money, people can now borrow and lend with confidence.

- **7A:** Allow entrepreneurs to borrow for projects (fishing nets, tools, etc.) → **Round 8**  
  Interest rates emerge as the price of lending. Time preference reveals itself in exchange rates. Capital formation begins.
- **7B:** Restrict borrowing / cap interest rates → slows capital formation; E10-adjacent outcome if continued.  
  Tools get built more slowly; productivity gains delayed.
- **7C:** Encourage very high-interest lending (predatory) → E4-adjacent risk; inflation and instability later.  
  Too much credit too fast; boom-bust cycle risk.

---

### **Round 8 — Entrepreneurship & Productivity (Innovation)**
**Terms: Productivity gain, technological progress, entrepreneurship (Schumpeter)**

(From 7A)

Capital is now available. Entrepreneurs take risks on new tools and methods.

- **8A:** Support and encourage entrepreneurship (low regulatory barriers, risk-friendly culture) → **Round 9**  
  Tools get built (fishing nets, irrigation, etc.). Production costs drop. Prices fall. Supply curve shifts right.
- **8B:** Suppress entrepreneurship (high taxes, licensing requirements, approval delays) → **E11 Failed Entrepreneurship**  
  Capital exists but nobody uses it; productivity stays flat; growth stalls.
- **8C:** Only allow elite to innovate (license monopolies to connected entrepreneurs) → E6-adjacent; growth for few, stagnation for many.

---

### **Round 9 — Consumer Markets (Demand Emerges)**
**Terms: Law of demand, price elasticity, Engel's Law**

(From 8A)

Productivity surge: goods are cheaper and more abundant.

- **9A:** Allow free consumer markets (people buy freely based on lower prices and higher incomes) → **Round 10**  
  Law of demand operates: lower prices → higher quantities demanded. Engel's Law visible: as incomes rise, food budget share falls. Demand pulls production.
- **9B:** Tax consumption heavily or ration goods → **E12 Demand Collapse**  
  Goods pile up unsold; farmers/makers lose incentive to produce next round. Supply collapses.
- **9C:** Control prices with price ceilings → shortages develop; black markets emerge; inefficiency.

---

### **Round 10 — Monetary Control & Inflation (Reserve Enforcement)**
**Terms: Fractional-reserve banking, inflation, quantity theory of money**

(From 9A)

Growth is exponential now. Temptation looms: can the banker issue more paper than gold to fund even more projects?

- **10A:** Strictly enforce reserve requirements (say, 50% reserves minimum) → **Round 11**  
  Money supply grows in proportion to real economic growth. Prices stable or slightly rising with productivity. Inflation controlled. **This is the load-bearing decision.**
- **10B:** Never enforce reserves; let banker issue freely → **E4 Inflation Crisis** (loops back to R0/barter)  
  Unbacked credit fuels a boom. Prices rise sharply. Trust shatters. Island reverts to barter. Nine rounds of progress unwound.
- **10C:** Over-enforce reserves; shrink money supply → **E17 Hyper-Deflation**  
  Money becomes scarce; people hoard; prices fall perpetually; investment dies; economy stalls in deflationary trap.

---

### **Round 11 — External Trade (Global Integration)**
**Terms: External trade, comparative advantage at scale, exchange rates**

(From 10A)

A second island appears, with different climate, resources, and skills.

- **11A:** Open trade freely with the second island → **Round 12**  
  Comparative advantage at the macro level: each island specializes, trade booms. Comparative advantage gains reapply. Exchange rates discovered or negotiated.
- **11B:** Impose protectionist tariffs and quotas; stay closed → **E2 Prosperous but Insular**  
  Internal economy works perfectly; growth is rapid but capped; never gets the external trade boost. Growth ceiling is ~40% below E1.
- **11C:** Open trade but with no agreed exchange rate mechanism → **E14 Fragmented Currencies**  
  Trade attempt fails; price signals don't translate; weak bilateral barter instead of vibrant market. External trade term appears distorted (⚠).

---

### **Round 12 — Commons & Sustainability (Resource Management)**
**Terms: Tragedy of the commons, resource management**

(From 11A)

The fishing ground (or other shared resource) is now under pressure from a larger population and growing external trade.

- **12A:** Regulate the commons (quotas, licensing, or rotation schemes) → **E1 The Prosperous Republic** ⭐  
  **All 12 rounds succeeded. All 15 economic terms functioning. Maximum wealth, low inequality, high stability, sustainable growth.**
- **12B:** Leave the commons unregulated → **E3 Commons Crisis**  
  Fishing ground gets fished out catastrophically. Years of compound growth erode from underneath. Productivity collapse. Growth reverses.
- **12C:** Regulate the commons late (after partial collapse) or weakly → **E15 Overexploited Commons**  
  Fish stock crashes before regulation kicks in. Partial recovery over years. Between E1 (full success) and E3 (collapse).
- **12D:** Regulate the commons AND maintain cautious limited trade → **E18 Subsistence Equilibrium**  
  Not quite as open as E1, but protected. Solid steady growth. Realistic, stable, mixed-outcomes scenario.

## Part 6 — Quick Reference: Teaching Guide

| Scenario | Best For Teaching… | Key Economics Lesson |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | Complete system | Institutional complementarity: every piece depends on every other piece |
| **E2** | Opportunity cost & gains from trade | Closed economies forgo massive wealth; trade is not zero-sum |
| **E3** | Market failures & externalities | Perfect markets fail on shared resources; regulation required for commons |
| **E4** | Central banking & monetary policy | Reserve enforcement is foundational; one policy error unwinds 9 rounds of progress |
| **E5** | Why property rights matter first | No incentives without ownership; specialization impossible without security |
| **E6** | Inclusive vs. extractive institutions | Narrow wealth capture is unstable; broad-based growth is both ethical and economically sound |
| **E7** | Importance of medium of exchange | Money is enabling technology; barter has a natural ceiling; growth is possible but throttled |
| **E8** | Why trust in currency matters | Fiat works only with earned credibility; decrees fail; institutions must evolve organically |
| **E9** | Crony capitalism & regulatory capture | Limiting access to markets concentrates gains; rent-seeking replaces productive competition |
| **E10** | Capital formation prerequisite | Without borrowing capacity, no tools; without tools, no productivity gains; no way to escape subsistence |
| **E11** | Entrepreneurship incentives | Productivity requires risk-taking; taxation or regulation that kills incentives kills innovation |
| **E12** | Supply is not enough without demand | Production must meet consumption; suppress demand and you suppress production next cycle |
| **E13** | Path dependency & irreversible decisions | One bad policy in Round 2 can undo 11 rounds of progress; order matters enormously |
| **E14** | Exchange rates & currency compatibility | Trade requires price signals; incompatible currencies break the feedback loop; technical standards matter |
| **E15** | Prevention vs. cure | Late regulation of commons is expensive; partial recovery takes decades; prevention is cheaper |
| **E16** | Institutional capture & hidden corruption | Rules on paper mean nothing if officials ignore them; trust erodes silently until crisis |
| **E17** | Deflation trap | Too little money is as damaging as too much; hoarding kills investment; money supply must match growth |
| **E18** | Realistic middle paths | Perfect decisions rarely happen; modest, stable success is realistic and valuable |

## Part 8 — Playable Game Flow (Narrative Arc)

### Act I: Foundation (Rounds 1–3)
**Theme: "From Survival to Specialization"**

Two to three islanders each produce everything for themselves. You, the government, must establish basic rules. Will property be secure? Will people be allowed to specialize? Will the market expand?

**Best outcome so far:** Free trade, open specialization, growing markets. Everyone's output rising. Simple barter still in use, but it works.

---

### Act II: Monetization (Rounds 4–6)
**Theme: "The Rise of Money and Trust"**

Gold arrives. Paper claims follow. The island's economy becomes more complex and more productive. But also more fragile—trust in paper money is critical.

**Best outcome so far:** Gold standard established, banking licensed, notes circulate backed by gold. Trust is high. Growth is compounding.

---

### Act III: Capital & Innovation (Rounds 7–9)
**Theme: "Tools, Risk, and the Frontier"**

Entrepreneurs borrow to build tools. Production methods improve. Prices fall. Consumption surges. This is the most visible prosperity phase—standards of living visibly rise.

**Best outcome so far:** Capital flowing freely, innovation happening, abundant goods, rising incomes. But money supply is growing—will it be controlled?

---

### Act IV: Global & Sustainability (Rounds 10–12)
**Theme: "Abundance, Fragility, and Stewardship"**

The critical phase. Inflation must be controlled or the system collapses. External trade opens. Shared resources come under pressure. Every institution must hold.

**Best outcome (E1):** All systems working, trade flourishing, commons protected, exponential growth sustainable.

**Worst outcomes:** Inflation destroys money (E4), commons collapse (E3), regulations captured (E16), growth throttled (E11, E12, E17).

## Part 9 — Common Patterns Across Endings

### **The "Institutional Complementarity" pattern** (E1)
All 12 rounds succeeded. No single term is missing. Result: exponential growth, low inequality, high stability. **Key lesson:** Institutions are not independent; they reinforce each other. Fix one in isolation and others fail.

### **The "Bottleneck" pattern** (E7, E10, E11, E17, E18)
Some institution works perfectly, but one bottleneck blocks the rest. E7: no money means no banking. E10: no banking means no capital. E11: capital exists but entrepreneurship is suppressed. E17: money is too scarce. All grow, but growth is capped.

### **The "Collapse" pattern** (E3, E4, E12)
Beautiful growth through most rounds, then catastrophic failure in one decision. E3: commons collapse. E4: money collapses. E12: demand collapses. Growth reverses sharply. **Key lesson:** End-state quality matters; you can undo 9 rounds of progress in one decision.

### **The "Loop" pattern** (E5, E6, E8, E13)
The decision sends the island backward to re-litigate an earlier round or stays permanently trapped at an early stage. E5 & E6: stuck at R1 (property rights). E8: loops back to R4 (money choice). E13: loops back to R1 (origin). **Key lesson:** Bad choices are sometimes irreversible; order is critical.

### **The "Extraction" pattern** (E6, E9, E16)
Growth is real but concentrated. Most of the population sees little benefit. Aggregate numbers look okay but distribution is terrible. Unstable in the long run.

## Part 10 — How to Use This in a Classroom or Game

### **As a lecture framework:**
Walk through R1–R12 sequentially. At each round, discuss the decision, explain the economic term, and ask: "What should we choose?" Have students vote. Show where each choice leads. By the end, students understand how 15 economic terms interconnect to create the structure of an economy.

### **As a playable game:**
Let students or players make choices R1–R12. The game engine shows them the growing economy at each step (population, output, living standards, prices, trade volume) and warns them of risks (inflation, inequality, commons stress). At R12, reveal the final scenario.

### **As a simulation with random shocks:**
After each round, introduce a random event (bad harvest, discovery of new resource, external invasion, plague). Force players to adapt their institutions to handle shocks. Fragile economies (those with low institutional quality) collapse under shock; robust ones (E1) absorb it and recover.

### **As a comparison tool:**
Pick two endings (e.g., E1 and E4) and trace backward: what were the critical decision points? What would have to change for E4 to become E1? This teaches causal thinking and path dependency.

## Part 11 — Design Notes for Game Developers

### **Difficulty levels:**
- **Tutorial (E1 path visible):** Highlight the "right" choice at each round. Player learns the system.
- **Normal (all paths open):** No highlighting. Player must experiment. Multiple endings discoverable.
- **Hard (random shocks):** After each round, a random event tests the economy. Poor institutions fail. Forces robust decision-making.

### **Playable terms:**
- **Population:** Grows each round (shows labor force growth term). Faster if economy is healthy.
- **Output (GDP):** Rises with specialization and capital. Visible growth curve. Sharp crashes in collapse endings.
- **Prices:** Fall when productivity rises (supply shock). Rise when money is over-issued (inflation). Fall and stay low in deflation. Players see QT of M at work.
- **Trade volume:** Rises with specialization, money adoption, external trade opening. Drops in protectionist/closed economies.
- **Gini coefficient (inequality):** Low in E1, E2, E7, E18. High in E4 (crisis), E6 (elite), E9 (crony), E16 (capture). Visible as wealth histogram.
- **Stability index:** Tracks fragility. Crises (bank runs, commons collapse, currency collapse) show as sharp drops.

### **Visual design:**
- **R1–R3:** Simple stick figures on island, trading goods. Increasingly complex trade network.
- **R4–R6:** Gold appears, banker's house appears, paper notes printed. Visual sophistication rises.
- **R7–R9:** Capital goods (nets, irrigation, workshops) appear on island. Production visibly organized.
- **R10–R12:** A second island appears (trade visualization). Commons resource (fishing ground) drawn and depleted/managed. Final state snapshot.

## Part 12 — Conclusion: The Island as Economic Parable

The Island Economy is a story about how complex systems emerge from simple rules. Start with two people and property, and the seeds of an entire economy grow naturally: trade, money, banking, capital, innovation, international commerce, resource stewardship.

But the system is fragile. Any institution poorly designed can break the whole. Property rights without property protections fail (E5). Trade without money gets throttled (E7). Money without banking stays limited (E7). Banking without reserve enforcement collapses (E4). Entrepreneurship without demand spirals into waste (E12). Global trade without commons management crashes (E3).

The game teaches:
1. **Order matters.** Property rights must come before specialization.
2. **Complementarity matters.** Institutions reinforce each other; one weak link breaks the chain.
3. **Incentives matter.** Every choice either encourages or discourages productive behavior.
4. **Robustness matters.** Systems that work in normal times can shatter under pressure. E1 is resilient; most others are fragile.
5. **Path dependency matters.** Early choices constrain later ones. Some decisions are hard to undo.

The Prosperous Republic (E1) is not the default outcome. It requires discipline, wisdom, and luck. Most paths lead somewhere else: slower growth, collapsed growth, captured growth, throttled growth, or growth that collapses at the moment of maximum vulnerability.

That is the game. That is the story of economies.
