# Island Economy — Story-to-Term Reference

A stage-by-stage map from your game narrative to the correct economic terminology.

| Stage | Event in the game | Core mechanic | Economic term(s) | One-line definition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2–3 people on the island, each producing everything they consume alone | No exchange | **Autarky** / **Self-sufficiency** ("Robinson Crusoe economy") | An individual or group produces all it consumes, with zero trade. |
| 2 | They start exchanging goods/services with each other | Specializing and trading | **Division of labor**; **Gains from trade**; **Comparative advantage** (Ricardo) | Splitting tasks by who's relatively best at what, then trading, makes everyone better off — even the person who's worse at everything. |
| 3a | A 4th and 5th person arrive, bringing new labor/skills | More workers | **Labor force growth** | More people = more potential output and more possible trades. |
| 3b | Their arrival lets tasks split even further, raising everyone's standard of living | Deeper specialization | **Extent of the market** (Adam Smith, *Wealth of Nations* Bk I, Ch. 3); **Productivity gains from specialization** | "The division of labor is limited by the extent of the market" — more people/trading partners allow narrower, more efficient specialization (Smith's pin-factory logic). |
| 4a | A 6th person introduces gold to replace direct barter | Money emerges | **Medium of exchange**; **Commodity money**; solves the **double coincidence of wants** | Gold is adopted as a universally accepted good-for-trading, removing the need to find someone who wants exactly what you have. |
| 4b | That person holds gold and issues claims against it, acting as banker | Financial intermediation | **Financial intermediary**; **Warehouse banking / gold depository** (if 1 claim = 1 unit of gold held) | Someone stores the commodity money and issues paper claims on it, standing between depositors and their gold. |
| 5 | The banker introduces paper notes redeemable in gold | Representative money | **Representative money**; **Gold standard** / **gold-backed note system** | Paper itself has no intrinsic value — it's valuable only because it's a redeemable claim on gold held by the banker. |
| 5b *(optional next step)* | Banker issues more notes than gold actually on hand | Credit expansion | **Fractional-reserve banking** | The banker lends/issues more claims than the reserves backing them, banking on not everyone redeeming at once. |
| 6a | Food becomes cheaper to produce | Productivity improvement | **Productivity gain** / **technological progress**; shifts supply curve right (positive **supply shock**) | Same labor now produces more output, or the same output for less input — lowering the market-clearing price. |
| 6b | People consume more food as a result | Demand response | **Law of demand**; **Price elasticity of demand** | As price falls, quantity demanded rises — elasticity measures how strongly. |
| 6c *(optional layer)* | As overall income on the island rises, the *share* spent on food falls even as amounts consumed rise | Budget-share shift | **Engel's Law** (Ernst Engel) | The proportion of income spent on food declines as income rises, even though absolute food consumption may increase. |

## Suggested arc order for the game
1. **Autarky** → 2. **Division of labor / comparative advantage** → 3. **Labor force growth** + **extent of the market** → 4. **Medium of exchange** (commodity money) → 5. **Financial intermediation** (banker) → 6. **Representative money / gold standard** → 7. *(optional)* **Fractional-reserve banking** → 8. **Productivity shock** → 9. **Demand response / Engel's Law**

This gives you a clean causal chain: *specialization → trade → population growth → deeper specialization → money → banking → representative money → productivity growth → consumption shifts* — each stage unlockable as a new "chapter" or mechanic tier.
