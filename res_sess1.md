# Island Economy — Full Story-to-Term Reference & Government Scenarios

## Part 1 — Story-to-term reference table

| Stage | Event in the game | Economic term(s) | One-line definition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 2–3 people on the island, each producing everything alone | **Autarky / self-sufficiency** ("Robinson Crusoe economy") | Producing all you consume yourself, with zero trade. |
| 2 | A basic rule about who owns what gets established (implicit or explicit) | **Property rights** | Recognized claims over resources/output — the precondition that makes voluntary exchange (rather than seizure) possible. |
| 3 | They start exchanging goods/services with each other | **Division of labor**; **Gains from trade**; **Comparative advantage** (Ricardo) | Specializing in what you're relatively best at, then trading, makes everyone better off — even the person who's worse at everything. |
| 4 | A 4th and 5th person arrive, bringing new labor/skills | **Labor force growth** | More workers = more potential output and more possible trades. |
| 5 | Their arrival lets tasks split even further, raising everyone's standard of living | **Extent of the market** (Adam Smith, *Wealth of Nations* Bk I, Ch. 3); **Productivity gains from specialization** | "The division of labor is limited by the extent of the market" — more trading partners allow narrower, more efficient specialization. |
| 6 | A 6th person introduces gold to replace direct barter | **Medium of exchange**; **Commodity money**; solves the **double coincidence of wants** | Gold is adopted as a universally accepted good-for-trading, removing the need to find someone who wants exactly what you have. |
| 7 | That person holds gold and issues claims against it, acting as banker | **Financial intermediary**; **Warehouse banking / gold depository** | Someone stores the commodity money and issues paper claims on it, standing between depositors and their gold. |
| 8 | The banker introduces paper notes redeemable in gold | **Representative money**; **Gold standard** | Paper has no intrinsic value — it's valuable only as a redeemable claim on gold held by the banker. |
| 9 | Someone borrows gold/notes to build a tool (e.g. a fishing net) instead of consuming today | **Capital investment / capital formation**; **Interest**; **Time preference** | Giving up present consumption to build a tool that raises future output; interest is the price paid for borrowing, rooted in people generally valuing goods now over goods later. |
| 10 | The new tool lets one item (e.g. food) get produced more cheaply | **Productivity gain / technological progress** (positive supply shock); often driven by **entrepreneurship** (Schumpeter) — someone taking a risk on a new idea/tool | Same labor now produces more output — shifting the supply curve right and lowering the market price. |
| 11 | People consume more of that cheaper item | **Law of demand**; **Price elasticity of demand**; **Engel's Law** (Ernst Engel) | Lower price → higher quantity demanded; Engel's Law adds that as income rises, the *share* of budget spent on food falls even as amounts consumed rise. |
| 12 | The banker issues more notes than gold actually held | **Fractional-reserve banking** → **Inflation** | Issuing more claims than reserves back; if trust breaks, too much paper chases the same goods and prices rise. |
| 13 | A second island or trading ship appears | **External trade**; **Comparative advantage at scale**; **Exchange rates** (if a different money is used) | The same specialization logic reapplied between two whole communities instead of individuals. |
| 14 | A shared resource (e.g. a fishing ground) comes under strain from overuse | **Tragedy of the commons** | A resource nobody owns individually gets over-exploited because no one bears the full cost of their own use. |

## Part 2 — Government point-of-view: eleven outcome scenarios

The player is the government watching over the island. Starting from a handful of self-sufficient islanders, the government faces five rounds of policy decisions, each offering 2–3 choices. Most choices push the story to the next round; some choices are dead ends that resolve into a distinct scenario; a few choices are *regressive* — they don't just fail to progress, they unwind progress already made and send the island back toward an earlier state, sometimes all the way back to bare autarky. The full round-by-round structure is in Part 3; below are the eleven scenarios the tree can resolve into.

### E1 — The Prosperous Republic (best ending)
Path: protect property rights → free specialization & trade → legalize gold → license the bank *and* enforce reserves → open trade with the second island *and* regulate the fishing ground. Every institution gets built in the right order and none of them gets abused. Growth compounds at every stage and the commons survives contact with a growing population.

### E2 — Prosperous but Insular
Same as E1 through sound banking, but the government regulates the fishing ground while declining to trade with the second island. The internal economy is completely sound — money, banking, capital, productivity — but the island never captures the gains that come from trading with a second community, so growth plateaus below what E1 reaches.

### E3 — Commons Crisis
Same as E1 through sound banking and external trade, but the fishing ground is never brought under any rule. Every other institution works exactly as intended right up until the moment a shared resource that nobody owns gets fished out, and the growth built over four rounds erodes from underneath.

### E4 — Inflation Crisis → Reset
Property rights, trade, and gold money all go well, and the bank gets licensed — but reserves are never enforced. The banker issues far more notes than gold on hand; the extra "credit" funds a short-lived investment boom, then trust collapses and prices spiral. Confidence in paper money is destroyed so thoroughly that the island reverts to barter, and in the worst cases to autarky — this is the branch where a decision made in round 4 of 5 unwinds everything, looping the story back to the round 1 starting state.

### E5 — Stagnant State: Insecurity
Property rights are never established at all. Without secure claims over resources or output, nobody has a reason to specialize or trade — anything you produce beyond immediate consumption can just be taken. The island stays in permanent autarky; population may still grow, but output per person never does.

### E6 — Extractive Elite State
Property rights exist, but only for a ruling clique — everyone else works land and tools they don't own. A thin layer of specialization, money, and trade exists among the elite, but it never broadens; most islanders live at subsistence while a few capture the gains. It looks like an economy but functions like autarky for most of the population.

### E7 — Closed Barter Economy
Property rights and free trade are both established, but gold is never legalized as money. Specialization keeps happening — but every exchange still needs a double coincidence of wants, so trade stays capped well below its potential. Real, permanent, but throttled growth.

### E8 — Fiat Overreach Crisis
Instead of building up from commodity money, the government decrees an unbacked paper token as legal tender before any banking trust exists to support it. The token is accepted only as long as the decree is credible; the first serious shock breaks that trust, and the island is forced back to relitigating round 3 — gold or barter — from scratch.

### E9 — Divided Island / Crony Trade
Trade is allowed, but licensed only to a favored subgroup. Comparative advantage gets exploited, but only by license-holders; the rest of the island is functionally still autarkic. Growth is real for a few and absent for most — a scenario the aggregate numbers can make look healthier than it is.

### E10 — Isolationist Relapse
Trade starts freely, then the government mandates self-sufficiency quotas — protectionism severe enough to dismantle division of labor that had already formed. This is the closest thing to a pure loop: households that had specialized get pushed back into producing everything themselves, landing the island back near its round 1 starting condition.

### E11 — Capital-Starved State
Property rights, trade, and gold money all work. But the government, wary of banker abuse, bans banking outright. Without anyone able to borrow, nobody can afford to build the tool (the fishing net) that would have raised productivity — capital formation never happens, and the economy stalls at a comfortable but permanently lower ceiling than E1.

## Part 3 — The five-round decision tree (arc used for the diagram)

Every round starts from the state the previous round's choice left behind. The "A" choice in each round is the one that keeps the island moving toward the Prosperous Republic; the other choices resolve into a scenario from Part 2, and five of them (marked **loop**) send the story back to an earlier round instead of forward.

**Round 0 — Origin.** 2–3 islanders, autarky, nothing owned.

**Round 1 — Property rights.**
- 1A Protect property rights → Round 2
- 1B Leave resources open-access → **E5 Stagnant State** (loop: reform retries Round 1)
- 1C Grant rights only to a ruling clique → **E6 Extractive Elite State** (loop: revolution retries Round 1)

**Round 2 — Specialization & trade.** (from 1A)
- 2A Allow free specialization & trade → Round 3
- 2B Mandate self-sufficiency quotas → **E10 Isolationist Relapse** — **loops back to the Origin**
- 2C License trade only to a favored subgroup → **E9 Divided Island**

**Round 3 — Money.** (from 2A)
- 3A Legalize gold as money → Round 4
- 3B Reject money, stay on barter → **E7 Closed Barter Economy**
- 3C Decree an unbacked paper token before any banking trust exists → **E8 Fiat Overreach Crisis** — **loops back to Round 3**

**Round 4 — Banking & capital.** (from 3A)
- 4A License the bank and enforce reserves → Round 5
- 4B License the bank, never enforce reserves → **E4 Inflation Crisis** — **loops all the way back to the Origin**
- 4C Ban banking outright → **E11 Capital-Starved State**

**Round 5 — External trade & the commons.** (from 4A)
- 5A Open trade with the second island *and* regulate the commons → **E1 Prosperous Republic**
- 5B Open trade, leave the commons unregulated → **E3 Commons Crisis**
- 5C Regulate the commons, stay closed to the second island → **E2 Prosperous but Insular**
